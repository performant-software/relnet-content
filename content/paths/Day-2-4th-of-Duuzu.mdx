The second day begins at the Eturkalamma, where wailers, cup-bearers, temple-enterers, and female courtiers lament while the opening riksu ša Bēl is recited. A mason performs recitations at the inner gate, and the cup-bearers and courtiers make a burnt offering, chant several compositions, and pour water to calm the fire devouring the jealous Zarpānītu.

The kurgarrû joins the cup-bearers and courtiers, emphasizing the ceremonies’ gendered character; Zarpānītu then blesses Marduk, followed by the bawdy “Eater(f.) of the stinking (plant),” reflecting the DLL’s contrast between the vulgar and the divine. The ritual moves from the Equlû to the Ekagula in the Esagil—likely passing through the city streets—then to a location near the Eturkalamma and into a park, where Bēltu searches for the gardener. At the “Gate of the Rābiṣu,” performers chant “ceremony! ceremony,” and “I heard she got laid” closes the rites of the 4th of Du’uzu.
