      The research identifies the people who participated in processions and pilgrimages, along with the divine entities involved in ceremonies, to better understand official religion's role in Babylonian society. It works across three main areas.

      First, we identify the people who — either as performers or spectators — participated in the processions within the cities or in the pilgrimages, and examine their roles and levels of participation. This helps clarify temple hierarchies and the responsibilities of different cultic participants.

      Second, the project examines the impact of the deployment of official religion in Babylonian society as a potential mechanism for reinforcing social cohesion and cultural identity.

      Third, we study the deities involved in ceremonies, their originating religious centers, and their ceremonial functions, mapping relationships between gods and goddesses in the Babylonian pantheon — revealing information beyond canonical religious texts such as the An = Anum list.

      The research applies a gender-perspective analysis to reassess these ceremonies, examining their performative dimensions and ideological significance.
