      It is important to study the movements, routes and itineraries that structured the contacts between different places of worship and the relationships between them. Religious networks operate in space and time; any movement has a beginning, a destination, a route and a duration.

      The research focuses on four key areas:

      - **Spatial positioning of worship sites** — whether they occupy central or peripheral locations — to understand religious geography in both urban and rural settings.
      - **Travel paths by land and water**, plus directional patterns of movement, to identify connections, network hubs, and hierarchical relationships.
      - **Distance factors**, intermediate waypoints, and related structures for travelers.
      - **Journey duration**, and whether trips follow liturgical calendars or recurring patterns.
