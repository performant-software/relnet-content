      RelNet examines how royal patronage shaped religious networks in late Babylon, focusing on three key areas:

      - Understanding the role of late Babylonian monarchs as temple patrons, and assessing the extent of royal backing for religious institutions.
      - Examining the participation of the monarch, members of his family or his entourage in processions and sacred journeys accompanying deities, such as during the Akītu festival.
      - Investigating the function of religious networks as instruments of political authority.

      The research emphasizes the importance of studying the different strategies of political legitimation created and reinforced through the dynamics of religious festivities in public places.
