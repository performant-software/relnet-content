      ## Religious networks

      **Sacred travelers and itineraries in late First Millennium BCE Babylonia: a view from temple and private archives (RelNet)**

      Cuneiform texts from institutional and private archives contain extensive documentation regarding religious networks in late first millennium BCE Babylonia, including their participants, venues and itineraries. Prior scholarship examining other regions and time periods demonstrates that analyzing religious networks proves essential for understanding the social and political significance of religious festivals and sacred locations, as well as the spatial dimensions of rituals and ceremonies. This remains a comparatively understudied area within Mesopotamian scholarship, though worthy of investigation.

      Contemporary research indicates that applying social network analysis (SNA) to religious phenomena helps move beyond rigid interpretations toward more relational, contextual and multifaceted understandings. However, this analytical approach has not previously been applied to Late Babylonian sacred landscapes. The Esagil functioned as a paramount political-religious center and primary connection point in the network linking Babylonian cult centers.

      Using approximately 500 cuneiform texts, the project establishes a database capturing network information, then examines religious festivals, journeys, venues, movements, itineraries and participants — paying particular attention to gender and cultural dimensions — using SNA and digital humanities tools. The project emphasizes integrating geospatial information with SNA, collecting and analyzing geographical data regarding cultic sites, travelers, movements and itineraries. Interactive maps constructed using geographic information systems (GIS) provide geospatial SNA visualization and enable broader public and academic access to findings.

      RelNet is based at the Department of History and Archaeology, University of Barcelona. Grant PID2022-136740NB-I00 funded by MICIU/AEI/10.13039/501100011033 and by the "European Union NextGenerationEU/PRTR".
