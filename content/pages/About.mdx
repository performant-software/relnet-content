  - title: Religious networks
    description: >-
      Cuneiform texts from institutional and private archives contain extensive
      documentation regarding religious networks in late first millennium BCE
      Babylonia, including their participants, venues and itineraries. Analyzing
      religious networks proves essential for understanding the social and
      political significance of religious festivals and sacred locations, as well
      as the spatial dimensions of rituals and ceremonies — a comparatively
      understudied area within Mesopotamian scholarship. The Esagil functioned as
      a paramount political-religious center and primary connection point in the
      network linking Babylonian cult centers.

    body: |
      Contemporary research indicates that applying social network analysis (SNA)
      to religious phenomena helps move beyond rigid interpretations toward more
      relational, contextual and multifaceted understandings — an approach not
      previously applied to Late Babylonian sacred landscapes.

      Using approximately 500 cuneiform texts, the project establishes a database
      capturing network information, then examines religious festivals, journeys,
      venues, movements, itineraries and participants — paying particular
      attention to gender and cultural dimensions — using SNA and digital
      humanities tools. Interactive maps constructed using geographic information
      systems (GIS) provide geospatial SNA visualization and enable broader public
      and academic access to findings.

      RelNet is based at the Department of History and Archaeology, University of
      Barcelona. Grant PID2022-136740NB-I00 funded by MICIU/AEI/10.13039/501100011033
